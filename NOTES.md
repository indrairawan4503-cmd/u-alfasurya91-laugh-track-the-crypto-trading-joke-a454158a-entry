# NOTES — Variant A: stop-loss / chart / portfolio turns on the trader

Concept: the stop-loss — the tool hired to protect the trader — does its job
so literally it ends up owning the trader's money. Named artifact (stop-loss)
→ literal application (guards the portfolio by trading) → human inverted
(the trader is reduced to watching the chart).

## Version log

- v1 (137 chars): "My stop-loss was supposed to protect me, so it sold my bags at the bottom, kept the rally for itself, and now it trades better than I do."
  Setup is clear, inversion is there, but "trades better than I do" is a soft
  punchline and "supposed to protect me" is throat-clearing.
- v2 (137 chars): "I set my stop-loss to guard my portfolio, so it sold the dip, bought back the top, and now it owns my money while I just watch the chart."
  Best: "guard" frames the job, "sold the dip, bought back the top" is the
  exact trader nightmare in six words, and the final inversion — it owns my
  money, I just watch the chart — lands at the very end. **PICKED.**
- v3 (112 chars, 114 bytes): "The chart finally broke out — straight through my stop-loss, my savings, and my will to ever check prices again."
  Funny list, but the chart is the actor, not the protector turning on the
  trader; the inversion is weaker and the em-dash costs bytes.
- v4 (116 chars): "My portfolio hired a stop-loss for protection, so it sold the dip, kept the profits, and fired me from my own money."
  Strong "fired me" ending, but "hired a stop-loss" is slightly less natural
  than "set my stop-loss", and it repeats v2's structure with less payoff.
- v5 (126 chars): "I put my stop-loss at 5% to stay safe, so the market dipped 5.1%, triggered it, and mooned while I watched from the sidelines."
  The 5%/5.1% detail is true to life, but it reads like a complaint about a
  wick, not a joke with a turn — no inversion of who owns what.

## Why v2 won
One concrete scene, one surprising turn, punchline at the end. Every word
earns its place: "guard" sets up the protector role, the middle delivers two
true trading details (sold the dip / bought the top), and the last clause
flips owner and watcher. A trader would repeat it to a friend. 137 chars /
137 bytes, inside the proven 100–170 byte range.
